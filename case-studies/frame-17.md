# FRAME 17
## A short-film concept about an image that tells the wrong story

**Format:** contained short, approximately 3–4 minutes as proposed. **Status:** unproduced concept and script sample. **Provenance:** original AI-assisted fiction prepared for this portfolio; not autobiographical, not a documentary claim, and not a finished film or acting credit.

### Logline

An archivist preparing a tribute discovers that an AI-restored handshake never happened. Removing it means losing the film's perfect ending—and finding the person the archive forgot.

### The story

A small screening room, hours before a local archive's anniversary event. Lena, an archivist, is checking a restored strip of silent film with the exhibition producer, Rowan. The new version ends on a handshake between the venue's two celebrated founders. It is exactly the image the exhibition needs.

Lena checks the frame counter. In the restoration, the hands meet. In the surviving contact sheet, frame 17 is torn. The generated version has completed a gesture across missing evidence.

Rowan argues that the handshake expresses a larger truth. The founders built the place together. Nobody is being harmed by a few recovered frames. Lena places the contact sheet beside the restoration and asks a smaller question: whose sleeve is entering from the edge of the original?

A print envelope holds a second, wider contact strip. In the intact neighboring frame, a woman takes the can at the projection booth. A caption identifies her as June, the projectionist. The wider sequence resolves the gesture: the hand was passing her a film can, not greeting a partner. The handshake exists only in the generated version.

The screening begins. Lena has removed the invented handshake. The damaged frame remains on screen long enough for the audience to notice it. A caption names June. The tribute is less seamless and more truthful.

### Scene sample

```text
INT. ARCHIVE SCREENING ROOM — EVENING

A handshake fills the screen. Smooth grain. Warm light.

LENA stops playback. Two hands hang an inch apart.

ROWAN
That's our ending.

LENA
It wasn't theirs.

She lays a contact sheet beneath the desk lamp.
Frame 17 is missing its center.

ROWAN
The restoration filled the gap.

LENA
It filled an argument.

Rowan leans over the sheet. Two men stand beside a projector.
At the edge: a sleeve, a wrist, the rim of a film can.

ROWAN
We know they worked together.

LENA
We know they were in the same room.

The projector fan keeps running.

Rowan turns over an old print envelope. A penciled note:
“June's copy. Return to the booth.”

ROWAN
Who's June?

A second contact strip slides from the envelope.
In its wider frame, a woman takes the can at the booth.
Beneath it: “June, projectionist.”

Lena aligns the two strips. The handoff matches.

LENA
The person they cropped out.

She advances to the damaged frame.

ROWAN
You're leaving that in?

LENA
Yes.

The gap becomes visible. So does the hand holding the can.
```

### Proposed shot sequence

| Shot | Image / action | Narrative function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A polished handshake pauses before contact | Introduce the tempting conclusion |
| 2 | Contact sheet under a hard desk lamp | Reveal the actual evidence gap |
| 3 | Matching screen and paper frame numbers | Make the comparison legible |
| 4 | A cropped sleeve and film-can rim | Turn a marginal detail into the central question |
| 5 | A second contact strip and its identifying caption | Resolve the handoff; identify the overlooked contributor |
| 6 | The damaged frame projected without repair | End on an editorial decision, not a lecture |

### Production approach

One interior location, two speaking performers and a small number of paper/film props keep the proposed shoot contained. The scene is built around a decision rather than spectacle. The reconstructed image must be labeled as an in-story fabrication in production notes; it must never be presented as a real archival find.
