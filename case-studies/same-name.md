# Same name, different people
## An identity-resolution exercise

**Type:** AI-assisted synthetic research exercise. **Status:** worked example, not a real family investigation. All people, dates, addresses and records below are invented. No archival search or DNA test was performed for this example.

### Research question

Does an 1891 marriage entry refer to Elias Vale born in 1854 (candidate A), or a second Elias Vale born in 1859 (candidate B)? An online tree favors B. The packet contains six record abstractions.

| ID | Invented record | Relevant information |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | Birth, 02/14/1854 | A; parents Joseph Vale and Anna Reed; Mill Street |
| R02 | Birth, 07/03/1859 | B; parents Samuel Vale and Ruth Lane; Station Road |
| R03 | Marriage, 06/18/1891 | Elias and Mara Holt; groom age 36; father Joseph; joiner; Mill Street |
| R04 | Household, 04/05/1891 | B, age 31, with wife Ada and father Samuel; Station Road |
| R05 | Online tree summary | Assigns the marriage to B; relies on a copied marriage index |
| R06 | Occupation entry, 08/11/1888 | A; joiner; Mill Street; father Joseph |

### Competing explanations

**A is the groom.** The father's name, address and occupation agree across R01, R03 and R06. R03's age of 36 conflicts by approximately one year with the age of 37 implied by R01 on the marriage date. That discrepancy must remain visible.

**B is the groom.** R05 supports the link, but contributes no independent observation. R02 and R04 associate B with a different father and address. R04 also places him with another spouse shortly before R03. A death, divorce, remarriage or clerical error could alter the interpretation; this packet supplies no such evidence.

**A third namesake or an error is involved.** The packet is not exhaustive. Two named candidates do not prove that only two people existed. A copied index may also misstate the original register.

### Bounded conclusion

Within this invented packet, A is the better-supported candidate because several identifying attributes converge across declared separate records. B's conflicting father and household details require unsupported additional explanations. The age discrepancy prevents silently treating every detail as resolved.

The appropriate output is a provisional identity link, accompanied by the conflict and the next decisive checks. It is not a percentage probability and not a claim to have met the full Genealogical Proof Standard in a real case.

### Next decisive checks

Inspect the actual marriage image, not just an abstraction. Look for witnesses and additional residence details. Seek A's intervening household or occupational records. Investigate B's marital history. Search systematically for other men with the same name before closing the case.

### Where Y-DNA would fit

No DNA data are present. In a real extension, a shared paternal-line result would be a separate type of evidence; it would not by itself choose a particular named ancestor from a document. FamilyTreeDNA notes that a shared paternal ancestor can lie far outside a recent genealogical timeframe. Testing and interpretation would need consent, lineage context and a question specific enough for the result to address. [Method references](../SOURCES.md)

### What this sample demonstrates

The structure of a cautious identity argument: candidate separation, correlation of attributes, common-origin checking, retained contradiction and a conclusion narrower than the story one might like to tell.
